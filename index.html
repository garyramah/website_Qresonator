How a resonator works — the field’s method, not Qresonator’s

The component that holds the photon

A resonator is a structure that stores energy in one oscillating mode and releases it slowly. In the circuit quantum electrodynamics work this page draws on, a microwave resonator stores photons and exchanges them coherently with a qubit. The resonator’s quality factor, Q, measures how slowly it loses energy: a high-Q resonator holds its energy longer and answers in a narrower band.

Step 1 · Couple

Couple

A superconducting qubit is placed next to a resonator — in Wallraff et al. (2004), a Cooper-pair box coupled to an on-chip cavity formed by a superconducting transmission-line resonator. When the qubit is tuned into resonance with the cavity, the system shows vacuum Rabi splitting — the signature that the strong-coupling regime has been reached.

Step 2 · Store

Store

In Paik et al. (2011), the qubit sits inside a three-dimensional waveguide cavity that suppresses decoherence while keeping the coupling to the control signal. The cavity stores the field the qubit couples to; qubit coherence times rose by more than an order of magnitude in this architecture.

Step 3 · Read out

Read out

When the qubit is detuned from the cavity, the resonator reads the qubit state out without destroying it — dispersive quantum non-demolition readout (Wallraff et al., 2004). In the three-dimensional architecture the cavity acts as the entanglement bus and the readout circuit (Paik et al., 2011).

The field

Three results that anchor this page

Independent field research — not Qresonator work

Three published results anchor this page. None of them is a Qresonator result, and none of them is a product benchmark. Each is cited in full.

1 · Circuit QED is born

Wallraff et al., 2004

Wallraff, Schuster, Blais, Frunzio, Huang, Majer, Kumar, Girvin & Schoelkopf, “Strong coupling of a single photon to a superconducting qubit using circuit quantum electrodynamics,” Nature 431, 162–167 (2004). DOI: 10.1038/nature02851 · nature.com

A superconducting two-level system (a Cooper-pair box) was coupled to an on-chip cavity formed by a superconducting transmission-line resonator. The experiment demonstrated the strong-coupling regime in a solid-state system: vacuum Rabi splitting, coherent superpositions between qubit and a single photon, and high-fidelity dispersive quantum non-demolition readout of the qubit state when detuned.

2 · Resonators above one million

Megrant et al., 2012

Megrant, Neill, Barends, Chiaro, Chen, Feigl, Kelly, Lucero, Mariantoni, O’Malley, Sank, Vainsencher, Wenner, White, Yin, Zhao, Palmstrøm, Martinis & Cleland, “Planar superconducting resonators with internal quality factors above one million,” Applied Physics Letters 100, 113510 (2012). DOI: 10.1063/1.3693409

Planar coplanar-waveguide resonators with internal quality factors above ten million at high microwave powers, and over one million at low powers — the best low-power results approaching two million at about one photon in the resonator — achieved by making very smooth, clean interfaces between the aluminum metallization and the underlying single-crystal sapphire substrate. These are internal quality factors of bare resonators, not qubit coherence times.

3 · A cavity that protects coherence

Paik et al., 2011

Paik, Schuster, Bishop, Kirchmair, Catelani, Sears, Johnson, Reagor, Frunzio, Glazman, Girvin, Devoret & Schoelkopf, “Observation of high coherence in Josephson junction qubits measured in a three-dimensional circuit QED architecture,” Physical Review Letters 107, 240501 (2011). DOI: 10.1103/PhysRevLett.107.240501 · arXiv:1105.4652

A transmon qubit in a three-dimensional waveguide cavity: qubit coherence improved by more than an order of magnitude — energy relaxation times up to 60 µs, dephasing times of 10–20 µs without spin echo, and a cavity lifetime of about 50 µs. The authors note: “Attaining sufficient coherence is a requirement for realizing a large-scale quantum computer.”

Scope note. What this section does not show: no item above is a Qresonator result, a sensing result, or a product benchmark. Sensing, networking, and dynamical machine-learning applications are not demonstrated by the work cited here. Wallraff et al. (2004) is cited for strong coupling and readout, not for high Q — its own resonator quality factor was modest; the high-Q evidence is Megrant et al. (2012) alone.
Where resonators matter — field level, from the cited work

Three places the resonator does the work

Quantum computing

The resonator couples qubits and reads them out — the strong-coupling regime and dispersive readout of Wallraff et al. (2004), and the three-dimensional cavity architecture of Paik et al. (2011).

Quantum memory and buses

The cavity stores photons and acts as an entanglement bus — the three-dimensional cavity of Paik et al. (2011) and the transmission-line resonator of Wallraff et al. (2004).

High-Q resonator engineering

Planar superconducting resonators with internal quality factors above one million at low powers — Megrant et al. (2012).
